# Audit, 2026-10-14: why 837 trials found nothing

Every number below is reproduced by `python3 python/audit_root_causes.py`.
No new strategy was run for this audit. History was not edited: corrections are
appended to the registry as `annotation` entries and noted in each script's docstring.

## The short version

Three things went wrong. Only one of them was bad luck.

1. **The search was spent in the wrong place.** 76% of all trials (638 of 837)
   tested price-and-volume patterns. That's the most crowded, most-studied and
   least-informative family there is. The project's strongest evidence came from
   economically motivated ideas (crypto funding carry, the overnight premium, index
   deletions), which together got 8% of the budget.
2. **The day-trading signals carry no detectable information.** Scored causally,
   at zero cost, 0 of 12 rules have a significantly positive return. The earlier
   story ("a real signal, just smaller than the spread") came from a
   per-day weighting that uses information from later in the day.
3. **The cost model was never measured.** The "measured" spreads came from an
   estimator that, on hourly bars, returns a noise floor regardless of the true spread.
   This didn't change any verdict, because of (2), but every cost figure built
   on it is void.

Most of the methodological errors the project found and fixed (two lookaheads,
a weighting leak, a frequency bug) biased results **toward** finding an edge. So
the null results are not an artifact of over-strict testing.

### 2. The day-trading rules have no signal, not a small one

Re-scored with `evaluation.clustered_mean`: per-trade mean, with standard errors
clustered by session, at **zero** cost.

| Rule | Trades | Gross per trade | t | Smallest effect this test could detect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| gap_down_fill | 116,896 | +13.6bp | 1.47 | 25.9bp |
| midday_reversal | 514,033 | +0.8bp | 0.31 | 7.0bp |
| vwap_reversion_long | 404,707 | +0.6bp | 0.26 | 6.2bp |
| volume_climax_long | 86,696 | −0.8bp | −0.34 | 6.2bp |
| power_hour_long | 526,975 | −1.9bp | −2.13 | 2.4bp |
| (7 others) | | −7.2 to −0.2bp | all \|t\| < 1.1 | 5.5–20.9bp |

The tests had the power to see a 6bp edge in most rules, and none was there.

Two errors produced the earlier impression of a signal:

- **Per-day weighting is a lookahead.** Splitting a day's capital across all of
  that day's signals requires knowing at 10:30 how many will fire by 15:30. For a
  dip-buying rule, many late signals means the market kept falling. On the
  replication ETFs, trades on days with one signal made +32bp, and trades on days
  with nine or more made −7bp. The weighting quietly shrank the losing days. It
  produced the "+9.6bp gross" in trial 38 and the 1.52 Sharpe in trial 41. The
  causal version on the same data has a Sharpe of −1.27.
- **Mismatched t-statistics.** `intraday_study.py` printed a per-trade mean next
  to a t-statistic for the per-session mean. For vwap_reversion_long those were
  t=5.11 against a correct t=0.26.

### 3. The cost model was noise

`bidask.edge` clips a negative estimate to zero unless `sign=True`. With seven
hourly bars, 47% of session estimates are negative, so the average of clipped
values is a floor of roughly 6–11bp whatever the true spread is. Evidence:

- The estimate is flat at about 11.5bp round trip from $20 stocks to $500 stocks.
  Real spreads shrink sharply with price.
- SPY reads 1.64bp. A one-cent spread on a $600 ETF is 0.17bp, and SPY almost
  always trades at one cent.
- Pooled correctly (the mean of signed squares, then the square root), about 40% of
  symbol-months are negative, and several sub-$50 names fall below one cent,
  which is impossible.
- Correlation with the one-cent floor across symbols: −0.03.

A live-quote check through yfinance was also tried and discarded: its bid/ask
fields returned stale quotes of 500–2,700bp.

**Affected:** trial 32, `cost_model.py` (Phase 8 scenarios),
`cost_first_screen.py`, and every intraday net figure in trials 32–42.
**Not affected:** the conclusions of 28 and 38–42, since there is no gross edge
to cost. The earlier daily studies used a different, volume-based cost model.

### 4. Sharpe and multiple testing

- **Mixed frequencies.** The registry scored every record as 348 weekly
  observations. Trial 42 (701 daily observations) printed "clears the bar" at
  0.859, when the correct daily bar was 1.921. Fixed: `record` now takes
  `--periods-per-year`.
- **Placeholder Sharpes.** 15 records store 0.0 to mean "refuted". That's not
  a measurement, and it can't be deflated. Fixed: `record` now refuses a Sharpe
  without `--n-obs`.
- **Tiny samples.** Three records carry a Sharpe on fewer than 30 observations,
  including the 1.98 from trial 8 (6 observations) and the 1.66 from trial 16 (22).
  `record` now warns.
- **Effective trial count.** 300 heatmap cells sharing one set of entries are
  not 300 independent tries. The luck bar is now shown at three values of N:

| N | Luck bar |
|---|---|
| raw trials, 837 | 1.219 |
| declared studies, 42 | 0.841 |
| information families, 7 | 0.528 |

The true value lies in between. Even at the most lenient bar, nothing gets a
pass, because the stored Sharpes that clear it can't be trusted as stored:

- Trials 20, 21 and 24 (crypto carry) clear 0.528, but on 42 observations.
  Trial 16 (short interest) also clears it as stored, but it was refuted on the
  full sample (Sharpe 0.24).
- Trials 2, 6 and 8 (price family) clear it with 1.47, 1.46 and 1.98, but trial
  8 has 6 observations, trial 6 has 47, and trial 2 contradicts itself: a 1.47
  Sharpe on 348 weeks implies t≈3.9, while its own note records t=0.71.

The registry needs its early records re-derived from saved return series
before any of these numbers can be deflated properly. That's listed as open work.

### 5. Benchmarks and exposure

- Same-stock, same-bar, every-session controls are the right benchmark for a
  timing signal, and they're kept. They measure information, not profit. Trial
  38 set its pass bar on that benchmark and "passed" two rules that lost money
  in every cell. The pass bar must be on causal net P&L.
- All 12 intraday rules are long-only. Their benchmark removes market drift, but
  a book trading them still carries market beta. Any future candidate should
  report its beta and its return after hedging.

## The research process from here

1. **Declare the mechanism first.** Who is on the other side of the trade, and
   why do they accept a worse price? (Forced sellers, hedgers, index funds,
   leveraged-ETF rebalancing, slow-moving capital.) A price pattern alone is not
   a mechanism.
2. **One primary specification per study.** Variants are counted, and a study
   declares its family.
3. **Score with `evaluation.py` only.** Per-trade or fixed-notional P&L, clustered
   inference, and a future-perturbation check on any sizing rule.
4. **Report gross, break-even cost, and minimum detectable effect.** A verdict
   must not hinge on an unmeasured spread. If gross clears the one-cent floor
   with confidence, then get real quote data before calling it tradeable.
5. **Hold out time, not just names.** Design on data before a declared cutoff,
   then run once on data after it. A strategy that passes then goes to a forward
   paper ledger before anything else.
6. **Report beta and capacity** alongside Sharpe.

## Data cleanup, 2026-10-17

The CHRD bankruptcy re-stitch distorted both trial 50 and trial 52. Each of the
66 rows flagged by the sweep was reviewed by hand:

- **6 real errors in 5 stocks** (CHRD, IVT, CLSK, INDV, ASTH), 4,109 rows in
  total, were moved to a `bars_quarantine` table with the reason recorded. For
  re-stitches and pre-listing segments, the whole segment before the jump was
  removed, because any return spanning the gap is fake.
- **60 rows were kept.** They were flagged for an open more than 50% from the
  close, but they are real crashes and surges (GME 2021-02-25, March 2020,
  Globe Life 2024-04-11, LEU 2014-07-01 on 100x volume). Deleting them would
  flatter every strategy.

After the cleanup, no one-day move above +300% or below −90% remains. The
derived features and labels tables were rebuilt, and all tests pass. `build.py`
re-applies the quarantine after every ingest, so a re-download can't bring the
rows back. A full backup taken first is at `data/warehouse_backup_2026-10-17.db`.
