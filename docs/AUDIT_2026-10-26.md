# Audit, 2026-10-26: why 852 trials found nothing, and what to do instead

Scope: the whole repository, read rather than summarised: the SQL feature and
label layers, the legacy backtester, the scoring module, the registry, the
deflated-Sharpe code, the lockbox, the tests, and 17 study scripts (the last
reviewed by a second reader and spot-checked). Nothing in the registry was
edited and no new trial was run. Every number below was produced in this audit
or is quoted from the registry with its trial id.

## 1. Executive diagnosis

**There is no evidence of alpha in anything this project has tested, and the
evidence that there is none is weaker than the project believes.** Both halves
matter.

Five reasons, ranked by how much they explain:

| # | Reason | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The hypotheses had low prior odds.** 76% of trials went to transformations of one price series (public technical rules) on free, survivor-only data. These are the most-studied and most-arbitraged signals there are. | Confirmed (registry families) |
| 2 | **The samples could not resolve the effects being sought.** An annualised Sharpe has a standard error of about 1/√years. 2014–2026 (12.7 years) can reliably detect a Sharpe of 0.70 at best. The 36–45 month out-of-sample windows of trials 56–57 can detect about 1.4–1.6. Published anomalies run 0.3–0.8. So "FAIL" on those windows mostly means "could not tell". | Confirmed (arithmetic; `deflated_sharpe.py --power`) |
| 3 | **The information set was wrong for the target.** Free daily OHLCV for today's ~1,500 survivors cannot test earnings, revisions, ownership or flows point-in-time, and it cannot measure costs. Yet the project has had WRDS access since trial 57: CRSP daily with delistings and closing bid/ask back to the 1980s, Compustat, I/B/E/S detail with announcement times, Thomson 13F, CCM links and Fama-French factors. Only trial 57 used them. | Confirmed (WRDS libraries queried this audit) |
| 4 | **Several scoring choices tested the wrong quantity.** Long-only rules were judged by raw Sharpe against a zero-return luck bar, so beta looked like skill. The GBM's target was absolute 5-day direction, so it learned market timing; its top two features are market-level. The daily-signal t-statistics were clustered by entry date although trades overlap for up to 60 sessions. | Confirmed (code; quantified below) |
| 5 | **There is no clean holdout.** Every bar from 2014 to 2026 has been searched repeatedly. The lockbox is forward-dated, about three weeks old, and at least two study families read past its seal. | Confirmed |

Plausible but not established: that genuine edges in this space have been
arbitraged away (consistent with everything, but indistinguishable from reason 2
at this sample size); and that costs killed real signals (contradicted for the
intraday rules, which have no gross signal at zero cost).

The project's bug-hunting has been unusually good. Most errors it found biased
results *toward* an edge, so the nulls are not an artefact of over-strict
testing. What it lacked was not rigour; rigour was applied to questions the
data could not answer.

## 3. Methodology assessment

**Can the current framework discover alpha? Not with the current data and
holdout design.** The controls are good. They are aimed at a regime where any
real effect is below the detection limit.

What should change:

1. **Split exploration from confirmation.** One global N = 852 applied to every
   new idea charges an economically new hypothesis for 600 candlestick
   variants. Keep the full count; never reset it. But structure inference in
   two stages. Exploration on a design period is cheap and counted. Then a
   small number of pre-registered confirmatory tests on data the search never
   touched, where the relevant multiplicity is the number of confirmatory
   tests. Require t ≥ 3 at the confirmatory stage (Harvey, Liu & Zhu 2016).
2. **Get a historical holdout that exists.** CRSP gives 1926–2025 survivorship-free.
   Design on, say, 1995–2012, confirm on 2013–2025, and say plainly which parts
   of 2014–2025 earlier trials already saw.
3. **Judge each strategy class by the right null.**
   - Market-neutral long-short: excess return, FF5+momentum alpha with HAC SEs,
     beta ≈ 0 checked, against a zero null.
   - Long-only timing or selection: alpha and Sharpe *difference* against the
     same-universe hold. Never raw Sharpe against the luck bar.
   - Absolute-return or overlay: Sharpe net of cash, with tail risk and max
     drawdown, and exposure-adjusted (a 30%-invested rule must not be credited
     for low volatility).
4. **Power before running.** Every declaration states the minimum detectable
   Sharpe for its sample (`deflated_sharpe.min_detectable_sharpe`). A test that
   cannot detect the published effect size is not run as a pass/fail test.
5. **Signal before strategy.** Rank IC and decile spreads with Newey-West SEs
   (`evaluation.rank_ic`, `newey_west_t`), then factor-neutral alpha
   (`factor_alpha`). Only then exits, sizing and costs. Exits are never tuned
   on the confirmation sample.
6. **Measure costs.** CRSP daily closing bid/ask gives a per-stock, per-day
   quoted spread for 1990s–2025. Use it in place of every modelled spread.

Metrics, by role:

| Role | Market-neutral | Long-only / directional |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | FF5+UMD alpha t (HAC), net of measured spread | alpha t and Sharpe difference vs same-universe hold |
| Acceptance | confirmatory t ≥ 3; same sign and ≥ ½ the magnitude out of sample; PSR(SR > 0) ≥ 0.95 net; positive in ≥ 2/3 of 3-year subperiods | the same, on excess over hold |
| Diagnostics | beta, factor loadings, turnover, break-even cost, capacity at 1% of ADV, top-5-period share, skew/kurtosis, max DD, Calmar | plus exposure % and time in market |
| Never primary | raw Sharpe, trade win rate, profit factor, Sharpe from overlapping or firing-date samples | raw Sharpe vs a zero-return bar |

## 4. Alpha research opportunities, ranked

Ranked by prior evidence × feasibility with data already licensed. No returns
are forecast here.

1. **Earnings information (PEAD, announcement return, analyst revisions).**
   Mechanism: slow, attention-limited incorporation of hard information. This
   is among the most replicated anomalies. Data: I/B/E/S detail with
   announcement *times*, CRSP daily. Risk: the project's earlier vendor-data
   version collapsed under momentum neutralisation (trial 15); I/B/E/S settles
   whether that was the data.
2. **Short-term reversal as liquidity provision, conditioned on liquidity
   stress.** Mechanism: Nagel (2012), market makers paid more when capital is
   scarce. Trial 49 saw the predicted VIX split descriptively on 209 weeks.
   CRSP 1990–2025 gives the power to test it properly. Very cost-sensitive;
   needs measured spreads.
3. **Economic-link momentum (customer–supplier, Cohen & Frazzini 2008).**
   Mechanism: limited attention across linked firms. Data: the
   `wrdsapps_link_supplychain` library is licensed. Distinct information from
   anything tested.
4. **Multi-signal market-neutral composite.** Only after ≥ 2 sleeves pass alone.
   Trial 37 showed equal-weighting dead signals dilutes live ones.
5. **Machine learning on fundamentals + price.** Last, and only on features
   shown individually informative.

Deprioritised, with reasons: more technical rules (exhausted); intraday rules
(no gross signal; no quote data); short interest (refuted, trial 16 →
`short_v2.json`); index deletions (trial 45); options-implied signals (no
OptionMetrics access); pairs trading on daily bars (crowded and cost-heavy; low
information per unit of effort).

## 6. Next five experiments

Each one is a single registry declaration, with variants counted.

### E1 (first). Earnings drift on I/B/E/S, survivorship-free

- **Hypothesis.** Among CRSP common stocks above the NYSE 20th size
  percentile, the top-minus-bottom decile of SUE earns a positive FF5+UMD alpha
  over trading days +2 to +61 after the announcement.
- **Mechanism.** Investors under-react to earnings news (Bernard & Thomas 1989).
  Attention and arbitrage limits slow the price adjustment.
- **Data.** `ibes.det_epsus` actuals with `anndats_act`/`anntims_act`;
  consensus = median of each analyst's latest estimate in the 90 days before
  the announcement; `wrdsapps_link_crsp_ibes`; `crsp.dsf_v2` (returns with
  delistings, `dlybid`/`dlyask`, `dlyopen`); `ff.factors_daily`.
- **Target.** Return from the open of day +2 (after-close announcements
  shifted one day) to the close of day +61.
- **Features.** SUE = (actual − consensus) / price at day −5. Secondary,
  declared now: EAR = CAR[−1,+1] vs the market.
- **Tests.** Monthly calendar-time decile long-short, value- and
  equal-weighted. FF5+UMD alpha with Newey-West (lags 3). Rank IC by month.
  Decile monotonicity.
- **Validation.** Design 1995–2012. Confirm once 2013–2025, which earlier
  trials 10–15 touched with different data; say so. Forward lockbox thereafter.
- **Costs.** Measured half-spread from closing bid/ask at entry and exit, plus
  borrow of 50bp/yr on shorts (more for the bottom size quintile).
- **Power.** Monthly series: MDE Sharpe 0.59 on 18 design years, 0.69 on 13
  confirmation years.
- **Reject if** the design-period alpha t < 3, or the confirmation alpha is
  ≤ 0 or below half the design alpha, or net Sharpe < 0.3.
- **Advance if** it passes all three, and the alpha survives within the top
  size tercile.
- **Effort.** 3–5 days, mostly linking and timing.

### E2. Reversal as paid liquidity, conditioned on stress

- **Hypothesis.** The weekly residual reversal (losers minus winners on 5-day
  return net of industry, top 1,000 by dollar volume, VW) is larger when
  VIX(t−1) is above its trailing 252-day median. The slope on the high-VIX
  indicator is > 0.
- **Data.** CRSP daily 1990–2025, VIX from CBOE (1990).
- **Tests.** Weekly long-short series; interaction regression with HAC
  errors; break-even spread.
- **Validation.** Design 1990–2010, confirm 2011–2021, with 2022–2026 excluded
  as seen (trial 49).
- **Costs.** Measured spread; this is the most cost-sensitive experiment.
- **Power.** About 1,000 design weeks; MDE Sharpe ≈ 0.55.
- **Reject if** the interaction t < 3 in design, or net-of-spread return in the
  high-VIX state ≤ 0.
- **Effort.** 2–3 days, mostly reusing the E1 data layer.

### E3. Customer momentum

- **Hypothesis.** A firm's next-month return is predicted by its principal
  customers' last-month return (Cohen & Frazzini 2008), after FF5+UMD and the
  firm's own industry return.
- **Data.** `wrdsapps_link_supplychain` (check the vintage and lag of segment
  disclosures: link known only after the 10-K date), CRSP monthly.
- **Tests.** Monthly quintile long-short alpha (HAC) and Fama-MacBeth slope.
- **Validation.** Design to 2010, confirm 2011–2025.
- **Reject if** design t < 3 or the confirmation sign differs.
- **Effort.** 3–4 days; link timing is the risk.

### E4. Analyst revision drift

- **Hypothesis.** The 1-month change in the median FY1 EPS estimate, scaled by
  price, predicts next-month returns with a positive FF5+UMD alpha and a
  correlation below 0.3 with E1's SUE.
- **Data.** `ibes.statsum_epsus`, CRSP monthly.
- **Tests and validation.** As E1.
- **Reject if** its alpha disappears when SUE and 12-1 momentum are added as
  controls.
- **Effort.** 1–2 days on top of E1.

### E5. Pre-declared combination

- **Hypothesis.** Equal-risk combination of only the sleeves that passed E1–E4
  has confirmation-period IR ≥ the best single sleeve × 0.8√K_eff.
- **Run only if** ≥ 2 sleeves pass. Otherwise the project's answer stands as
  "no edge in licensed academic data at daily–monthly horizons".
- **Then** forward paper ledger per the existing forward-test policy.

E1 goes first. It has the highest prior of any untested hypothesis, uses data
already licensed, its infrastructure (CRSP daily, linking, measured spreads,
factor attribution) serves E2–E5, and it directly resolves the project's best
open question: was trial 15's collapse the vendor data or the signal?

## 8. Final assessment

1. **Is the backtester sound?** The newer scoring path (`evaluation.py`,
   registry, lockbox) is sound in design. The legacy backtester had two real
   execution bugs, now fixed, and several study engines carry known biases
   that are documented. Inference for overlapping trades was wrong until now.
2. **Are historical results trustworthy?** The *nulls* are trustworthy: errors
   mostly favoured finding an edge. Reported significance and power are not.
   Negative t-statistics are inflated about 2×, and short out-of-sample
   windows had no power to reject published effects.
3. **No alpha, or bad testing?** No alpha in what was tested. The testing was
   largely fair. But the tests could not have detected the effect sizes that
   plausibly exist.
4. **Wrong markets, horizons or data?** Wrong data, mainly. Horizons were
   explored (intraday to monthly) without result.
5. **Single biggest weakness.** Low-prior hypotheses tested on samples too short
   to detect realistic effects, with no untouched historical holdout.
6. **Stop.** New technical-rule variants; parameter sweeps; any study on the
   yfinance survivor universe; raw-Sharpe judgments of long-only rules;
   pass/fail verdicts from windows under ~10 years.
7. **Start.** CRSP/I/B/E/S data; power analysis at declaration; design and
   confirmation periods; factor-neutral alpha as the primary metric; measured
   spreads.
8. **Path to a defensible net Sharpe above 1.5.** Not from any single
   published daily anomaly; post-publication Sharpes are typically well under
   1. The only credible route is several economically distinct, individually
   confirmed market-neutral sleeves with low correlation, combined. Even that
   is an aspiration to test, not a forecast.
9. **Evidence of genuine alpha.** A pre-registered hypothesis with a stated
   mechanism. Alpha t ≥ 3 after FF5+UMD and industry. The same sign and at
   least half the magnitude on an untouched period. Net of measured spreads
   and borrow. Not concentrated in a few months. Stable across size groups and
   subperiods. Then a forward record consistent with the backtest.
10. **First change with institutional capital.** Move the project onto the
    WRDS data it already has, and re-base the protocol on design and
    confirmation periods with power stated up front.

### What the reruns found beyond the planned fixes

- The clustering correction is real but smaller than section 5 says. On the
  quarantined data, entry-date clustering gives 36 signals with |t| ≥ 2
  (median |t| 5.1). Quarter clustering gives 28, and 168-day blocks give 25
  (median 2.4), a factor of about 2. "8 survive" does not reproduce; it
  appears to have been computed on pre-quarantine trades, whose outliers
  inflate the standard errors.
- `reports/robustness_ta.csv` (item 12) reproduces exactly, 41 of 41 trade
  counts, only when the 4,109 quarantined rows are put back. It was written
  before the 2026-10-17 quarantine. So was `ta_exits.csv`.
- Trials 29-31 were recorded from outputs produced before the 10-13
  causal-volume fix, and were never annotated for it. Trial 31's

### Open design issue

The legacy GBM (`model.py`, frozen `gbm_v1`) has as its target absolute 5-day
direction (`label_win_5d`). So it learns market timing, and its most important
features are market-level. Not changed: `gbm_v1` is frozen and in a forward
test. A replacement should predict return relative to the market or the
cross-section.

`gbm_v1` was also trained on the old `dist_52w_*`. Their median at 2014-12-31
was −0.27, against −0.05 correctly built; by 2026 the two agree (median
difference 0.002). Its training inputs therefore differ from what it now sees
live. The rebuild barely moves its live inputs, so the forward test is not
disturbed further. The skew is in what it learned.

Decision (2026-10-26): `gbm_v1` keeps running unchanged as the control it
already is, with this skew on record. It is not retrained.
