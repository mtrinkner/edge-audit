## CRITICAL — Survivorship bias in the universe

**Where:** `symbols` table, `inclusion_reason` column.

```
S&P smallcap600 constituent as of 2026-10 (survivorship bias)      601
S&P 500 constituent as of 2026-10 (survivorship bias; documented)  503
S&P midcap400 constituent as of 2026-10 (survivorship bias)        399
```

Every company that went bankrupt, was acquired, or was dropped from an index
between 2014 and now is absent. The schema is honest about it, which is the right
place for the admission, but it contaminates every cross-sectional result.

**Direction:** inflates, and most severely for anything that buys losers.

**Evidence of the magnitude:** the turn-of-month run produced a mean-reversion
book at +27.26% annualised. It buys the bottom decile of 5-day returns, a median
loss of -7.38%. Every such stock that kept falling and left the index is missing.
That number is not reported as a finding anywhere, for this reason.

**Also demonstrated directly:** the federal-contract work found that RTN, UTX,
COL, HRS, AJRD, ORB and ESL have no price history at all, because the universe is
built from current constituents. $172.8bn of Raytheon contract actions had to be
dropped. Survivorship is not an abstraction here; it has been measured.

**Fix:** Phase 2. Three labelled universes, with coverage documented, and no
claim that a liquid universe is therefore unbiased.

## LOW (downgraded from HIGH after measurement) — The cost model uses information from after the trade

**Where:**
- `python/intraday_control.py:61` `d["sess_dollar"] = g["volume"].transform("sum") * d["sess_final"]`
- `python/intraday_passive.py:89`, `python/intraday_vix.py:95`, `python/edge_spreads.py:102` — same pattern.

The spread is priced from the session's **total** dollar volume, then charged to a
trade entered mid-session. At entry that total is not known.

**Direction:** inflates, in principle. Signals fire on high-volume bars, which sit
in high-volume sessions, so the modelled spread should be too tight exactly where
it matters.

**MEASURED MAGNITUDE: 0.06bp.** I rated this HIGH from the direction of the bias
without measuring its size, which is the error this project exists to catch, so
the rating is corrected here rather than quietly.

    all bars            as coded 10.39bp   causal 10.39bp   diff +0.01bp
    2x-volume bars      as coded 12.36bp   causal 12.43bp   diff +0.06bp
    (781,805 bars)

The leak is real and immaterial. The reason is that the spread model is a
function of the square root of dollar volume, and daily volume is strongly
autocorrelated, so the prior session is an excellent proxy for the current one.
A sub-basis-point error does not change any conclusion in this project, where the
smallest gap that mattered was 0.67bp and the typical one was 10bp.

**Fix:** still worth doing, because correctness should not depend on a bias
happening to be small, but it is reclassified from a correction to a tidy-up.
Regression test: assert the cost series is unchanged when future bars are mutated.

## HIGH — Short positions are never charged borrow

**Where:** `python/config.py:48` declares `borrow_bps_annual: float = 50.0`.
It is referenced nowhere else in the codebase.

`index_v1`, the one frozen strategy currently recording a live forward book, is
short: `python/freeze_strategy.py:106` "short the name, long SPY in equal dollar
amount". It has never paid borrow.

**Direction:** inflates. At 50bp annual over a 10-session hold the omission is
about 2bp per event, against a measured +1.2% per event, so it is small. But it is
unmodelled in the only thing running live.

**Fix:** apply the config value in position-level cost accounting; assert in a test
that any book with negative weights incurs a borrow charge.

## MEDIUM — Every execution cost is estimated; none is observed

No observed bid-ask quote exists anywhere in the project. Costs come from the
EDGE estimator (`python/edge_spreads.py`) or the older volume heuristic
(`python/spread_estimator.py`).

This has already changed a conclusion once. The volume heuristic had spreads
**narrowing** under stress, because volume rises; measured spreads **widen**.
That single sign error made a losing intraday strategy look like breakeven.

EDGE is also known to overstate mega-cap spreads here (3.35bp at the top decile
against a true ~0.3-0.5bp), so the true cost sits between the two estimators.

**Fix:** Phase 8. Separate estimated effective spread, quoted spread, realised
spread, impact and fees. Report every candidate under four cost scenarios. Never
present an estimate as an observed price.

## A false positive, recorded because audit precision matters

The first version of `tools_audit.py` flagged `transform("last")` as lookahead in
three modules. It is not: the session's final close is the **exit price and the
outcome**, not a decision input. A trade's exit being later than its entry is the
nature of a trade.

The pattern was too broad, and a tool that cries wolf gets ignored. The real leak
was hiding one line below it, in how that same groupby priced the **cost**. The
audit now distinguishes decision-time inputs from outcome-time quantities.

This is the same failure mode as the first intraday lookahead test, which passed
a rule already proven contaminated because the rule never fired in its sample.
Both are cases of a test that could not have failed.

# Phase 2 — Point-in-time universe: result and a trap

`python/pit_universe.py` reconstructs S&P 500 membership from Wikipedia revision
history, 26 semi-annual snapshots, 2014-01 to 2026-07. All 26 succeeded.

| | |
|---|---|
| distinct tickers ever a member | **798** |
| members today | 502 |
| deleted by a current-constituent universe | **296 (37%)** |
| of those, priceable with existing bars | 95 (32%) |
| **unpriceable** | **201 (68%)** |

So the survivorship hole is now measured rather than assumed: **37% of every name
that passed through the index during the window is absent from the universe all
prior research used.**

## The trap: the recoverable half is not a random half

    priceable    AA, AAL, AAP, ALK, AN, ATI      still trade; DROPPED from the
                                                 index, usually for weak performance
    unpriceable  ABC, ABMD, ACE, AET, AGN,       ACQUIRED, typically at a premium,
                 ALTR, ALXN                      then delisted

Filling in the 95 adds back mostly underperformers while the 201 still missing are
mostly acquisition winners. A naive partial correction would therefore bias long
strategies DOWNWARD, replacing an optimistic bias with a pessimistic one and
feeling rigorous while doing it.

**A partial survivorship fix can be worse than none when the recoverable subset is
systematically different from the unrecoverable one.** That is the case here, and
it is the reason `pit_sp500.json` is published as a diagnostic rather than wired
into the backtester.

## What this changes, concretely

1. **Universe A (current constituents)** remains the only one with complete price
   coverage, and is now labelled with a measured bias of 37% missing names rather
   than a general warning.
2. **Universe B (point-in-time membership)** exists and is correct as a MEMBERSHIP
   record. It cannot be traded without prices for 201 names.
3. **Universe C** is not created. Restricting to liquid names does not address
   this; several of the missing are mega-caps at the time of removal (AET, AGN,
   ALXN were all large index members).

## What would actually close it

Price history for delisted US equities. The free vendor serves currently listed
symbols only. This is the single data purchase that would most improve the
project's validity, and it is a purchase, not an engineering task.

Until then the honest position is: the universe is biased, the bias is 37% by
name count, the direction is optimistic, and the correction available for free
would introduce a second bias in the opposite direction.
